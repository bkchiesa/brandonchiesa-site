Peter Rucker is the earliest proven ancestor of our Rucker line, yet he still keeps most of his secrets.

## A petition in Williamsburg

The first record of Peter comes from the spring of 1704. On Monday, 24 April, the governor's Council in Williamsburg read "the petitions of Cyprian Prou, Peter Rucker, & Isaac Garret praying for Naturalizacon." They sent them on to the House of Burgesses. On 8 May 1704 a committee reported on his petition and those of John Gill, Stephen Gill, Samuel de Monville, Teleife Alverton, Isaac Garret and Cyprian Prou. Its finding was that it was "reasonable the said persons should be naturalized when they are qualifyed by taking the Oathes Enjoyned by Law." The House agreed.

Naturalization was only needed by someone born outside the English crown's dominions. So we can say with confidence that Peter was a foreigner. He was most likely born somewhere in continental Europe.

## French refugee, or something else?

Family tradition, recorded in 1932, says Peter was a French Huguenot who was shipwrecked near the mouth of the James River and swam ashore. The journals do not support the Huguenot part as firmly as older books suggest. The Council recorded the petitions of the French refugees at Manakin Town as a separate matter. Peter's petition was entered separately, with two other individuals. He is never called a refugee. His name is also missing from the 1705 act that naturalized the Manakin settlers. His surname looks Germanic, and one London expert of the 1930s thought it German or Dutch. Searches of Walloon and German Huguenot records have found nothing.

## What his DNA says

Male-line descendants who have tested with the Rucker Family DNA Project share a distinctive Y-DNA lineage, R-A434. It belongs to a western European branch called DF27. So far it does not match the German Rucker families who have tested, and no match has turned up in Europe. Peter's homeland therefore remains an open question.

## A planter on the Rappahannock

By 1704 Peter held 500 acres in Essex County. In January 1707/8 Frederick and Sarah Coghill leased land on Cockleshell Creek to Peter and his wife Elizabeth. The same day they deeded it to his son John, with his sons Peter and Thomas next in line. He later moved west to St. Mark's Parish in Orange County. There he made his will on 18 January 1743/4, signing with his mark. He left his estate to his wife Elizabeth for life, with bequests to his children Thomas, Elizabeth Pierce, William, Mary Offell, James, Ephraim, Ann Cook and Margaret Tinsley. The will was proved on 23 February 1743/4.

## What we do not know

No record names Peter's parents, his birthplace or the year he arrived. Online trees that give him a father named Ambrose Rucker, a mother named Elizabeth Beauchamp, or a birthplace in Bavaria cite no original records. They should be treated as unproven.
